Conversation analysis is a module dedicated to the comment section of posts from the company's own pages (official pages). Here you can see all the comments on the company's pages and analyze their sentiment, emotion and more.

### [1\. Go to Conversation Analytics tab and click on Conversation Analytics icon](https://dimaapp.darwinz.ai/en/brands)

### [3\. dima provides several graphs for the analytics tab](https://dimaapp.darwinz.ai/en/conversation-analysis?h_aWQ9NjY4)

Each vertical bar means one day.

The bar is split into colored segments that always add up to 100%. So for Sentiment, every bar is made of green (Positive), red (Negative), and orange (Neutral) stacked on top of each other.

The left Y-axis (0–100%) measures the height of those colored segments.

The right Y-axis (0–80) measures the blue line, labeled "Total Volume" — this tracks the actual number of replies that day, completely separate from the percentages.

Hovering over a bar pops up the exact breakdown for that single day.

The Emotion chart on the right works identically, just with 7 emotion categories (Joy, Trust, Surprise, Sadness, Disgust, Anger, Anticipation, Fear) instead of 3 sentiment categories.  
You can also add the graph to dima's AI chatbot for further information via hovering on the desired graph and then clicking on the icon labelled with the red arrow[this applies for any graph which shows the icon when the graph is being hovered over].

### [4\. For some of the graphs you can click on the engagement/volume button located on the top right corner of each graph](https://dimaapp.darwinz.ai/en/conversation-analysis?h_aWQ9NjY4)

Same exact structure as in previous step, but this is measuring Posts instead of Replies.

Notice the toggle at the top: "Volume" vs "Engagement", this switches what the blue line represents. Right now "Engagement" is selected, so the line tracks engagement instead of raw post count.

### [5\. This is the Reply Word Cloud](https://dimaapp.darwinz.ai/en/conversation-analysis?h_aWQ9NjY4)

This just displays whichever words or hashtags appear most often, bigger and bolder text means it is mentioned more frequently.

Toggle at top-left switches between showing Words and Hashtags.

Toggle at top-right ("Normal / Sentiment / Emotion") changes the coloring: Normal is just a neutral color scheme, while Sentiment/Emotion would color-code each word by whether it's associated with positive/negative or with a specific emotion.

"Stop Words" (top right button) lets you exclude common/unwanted words from ever appearing in the cloud.

### [11\. Reply Sentiment/Emotion per Topic](https://dimaapp.darwinz.ai/en/conversation-analysis?h_aWQ9NjY4)

Each slice of the pie charts shows one category, and the number inside the slice is the actual count (not a percentage).

The dropdown at top-left ("Smartphones" / "Consumer Financing") lets you filter the whole chart to just one specific topic so you're seeing sentiment/emotion only for replies related to that topic.

The two icons in the top-right corner (circle icon vs. the highlighted one) are view toggles to switch between a pie chart view and a bar/column chart view of the same data.

The legend below lists each category with its color and count so you can match it back to the slices.

![](https://d3q7ie80jbiqey.cloudfront.net/media/image/zoom/1d9a13ad-92ae-49a5-b92e-80b88d6d7db5/1/49.531249205271/17.643836034487?0)

### [12\. Reply Sentiment/Emotion Distribution](https://dimaapp.darwinz.ai/en/conversation-analysis?h_aWQ9NjY4)

The "Reply Sentiment Distribution" chart shows all the replies sorted into three groups: Positive, Negative, and Neutral so you can see the overall reaction at a glance.

Each slice of the pie is one group, and the number inside the slice is the actual count of replies, not a percentage.

The "Reply Emotion Distribution" chart breaks the same replies into eight specific emotions: Joy, Trust, Anticipation, Surprise, Sadness, Anger, Disgust, and Fear. So you can see what people were actually feeling.

The two icons in the top-right corner of each chart are view toggles that switch between a pie chart view and a bar chart view of the same data.

The legend below each chart lists every category with its color and count, so you can match each slice back to what it represents.
